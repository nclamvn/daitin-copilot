Overview

The patent registration process in Vietnam, similar to practices in numerous
other countries, consists of several stages of examination. During this process,
applicants may find it necessary to make amendments to their patent
applications. These revisions can help clarify, refine, or narrow the scope of
protection sought, ensuring that the application aligns more closely with the
actual patented innovation.

Daitin & Associates offers detailed analyses of the rights and limitations
concerning patent application amendments within the framework of
Vietnamese law.

Amendment Rights for Patent Applications Under Vietnam’s IP
Law

Under Vietnam’s Intellectual Property (IP) Law and Circular No. 23/2023/TT-
BKHCN, patent applicants are granted specific rights to amend their
applications at various stages of the patent process.

1. Before Publication: Applicants can proactively request amendments to their
patent applications before the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam (IP
Vietnam) publishes their application in the Industrial Property Gazette. This
early stage allows for greater flexibility in making changes as long as the nature
of the invention remains unchanged.

2. During Formality Examination: If IP Vietnam issues a notice indicating formal
deficiencies in the application, applicants are allowed to rectify these issues
within a specified period, typically two months from the notice date.
Amendments at this stage are primarily focused on meeting formal
requirements related to document formatting, fees, and other administrative
aspects.

3. During Substantive Examination: This stage is crucial, as it involves a
detailed assessment of the invention's compliance with protection criteria—
such as novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability. Should IP Vietnam
send a notice of substantive examination results suggesting a potential refusal,
applicants have the right to respond or amend their application within three
months. Amendments may involve clarifying specifications, narrowing claims,
or modifying them to satisfy protection requirements.

4. After Intended Grant Notice: Even after receiving a notice of the intended
grant from IP Vietnam, applicants can still request amendments or
supplementary information. In such cases, the application will undergo re-
examination.

5. During First-instance Appeal: Applicants who disagree with IP Vietnam’s
decisions (such as refusals of application acceptance or protection title grants)
have the right to appeal. However, per Article 35.3(a) of Circular No.
23/2023/TT-BKHCN, any request for amendments or supplements to a patent
application that has been refused will not be accepted during the appeal
process. Consequently, applicants in the appeal process before IP Vietnam are
generally prohibited from amending their applications unless specific provisions
in international treaties to which Vietnam is a party allow for such changes.


In summary, understanding these stages and the corresponding amendment
rights is essential for patent applicants navigating the process in Vietnam.

Limitations and Requirements

In Vietnam, while applicants have the opportunity to amend their patent
applications, this right comes with certain limitations aimed at promoting
transparency and fairness within the patent protection system.

One of the key principles governing these amendments is that the nature of the
subject matter disclosed in the original application must remain unchanged.
According to Clause 2, Article 102 of the Intellectual Property Law and further
clarified in Article 34 of Circular No. 23/2023/TT-BKHCN, any amendments
must not introduce new technical information or features that were not explicitly
disclosed in the original specification, claims, or accompanying drawings. This
means that adding new features or characteristics or expanding the scope of
protection beyond what was initially disclosed at the time of filing is prohibited.

The scope of permissible amendments is limited to clarifications of content,
narrowing the claims to meet protection criteria, or correcting typographical or
technical errors that do not change the essence of the invention. Typically,
expanding the scope of protection after a certain point in the application
process—especially after Publication—is not permitted.

Additionally, any amendments must adhere to formal requirements, meaning
they need to be submitted in writing using the designated forms, signed by the
applicant or their authorized representative, and include any necessary
supporting documents.

It is important for applicants to note that amendments must be submitted within
the time limits specified by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. Failing
to meet these deadlines could result in the refusal of the application..

Conclusion

The amendment of a patent application plays a vital role in the registration
process, allowing applicants to refine the scope of protection they seek while
ensuring adherence to legal standards. In Vietnam, applicants have the option
to amend their applications at different stages of the process. However, this
right comes with specific limitations, particularly the essential principle that any
amendments must not change the core essence of the invention as it was
originally disclosed.
